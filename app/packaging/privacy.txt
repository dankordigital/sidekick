SIDEKICK PRIVACY POLICY
Effective 5 October 2026 (updated 15 November 2026 for Sidekick 4.0)

THE SHORT VERSION

Sidekick has no account, no servers, no ads and no analytics. Your files go
straight between your own devices, encrypted. Nothing is sent to us. The
website's wishlist keeps only the email address you give it, to tell you
when Sidekick is out.

WHAT SIDEKICK KEEPS ON YOUR DEVICE

- Your settings: the device name, app icon, sound, the folder for
  received files, and what paired devices may do.
- Each device's Chat: a list of what you sent each paired device and
  received from it (file names and sizes, links, notes and codes), up to
  500 per device, so you can find them again. It stays on this device and
  is deleted when you unpair that device.
- For the home-screen widgets (iPhone and Android): the names of up to
  three paired devices, whether each can be reached, and the name of the
  last file you received, in storage only Sidekick and its widgets can
  read, so the widgets can show them.
- Your paired devices: each one's name, device ID, certificate fingerprint,
  and the keys that let the two of you talk securely. These are kept in the
  system's protected storage: the Keychain on iPhone, storage encrypted with
  the Android Keystore on Android, Windows' built-in data protection on
  Windows, a file only your user account can read on the Mac, and the
  desktop's keyring (Secret Service) on Linux. On Linux, "Open when you
  log in" is a small file in ~/.config/autostart.
- Its own security identity: a certificate and private key made on the
  device when Sidekick first starts.
- Files you receive, in the folder you choose. On iPhone and Android,
  received photos and videos go to Photos or the gallery instead (on
  Android, in Pictures/Sidekick and Movies/Sidekick).

None of this is uploaded anywhere. Removing Sidekick removes it, except the
files you received, which stay in their folder.

WHAT SIDEKICK SHARES, AND WITH WHOM

Nearby devices. So your devices can find each other, Sidekick announces
itself on your local network and over Bluetooth: its device name, a random
device ID, the kind of device (for example Mac or Android), its Sidekick
version, its network port, the fingerprint of its certificate, which
features it offers, and (over Bluetooth) whether it's on Wi-Fi. Anyone on
the same network or within Bluetooth range can see this announcement. It
contains no files and no other personal content.

On iPhone and Mac, Sidekick also announces its random device ID over
Apple's peer-to-peer Wi-Fi (the link AirDrop uses), so two Apple devices
can reach each other with no shared network. Apple devices within Wi-Fi
range can see that ID; anything sent over the link is encrypted, like on
your network.

On Windows and Mac, closing Sidekick's window keeps it running in the tray
(the menu bar on a Mac), so your devices can still reach it; choose Quit
there to stop it. With Auto-load (on unless you turn it off in Settings), it
also starts in the tray when you log in: Windows lists it under startup
apps, and the Mac keeps a small login item in your Library's LaunchAgents
folder. Phones keep Sidekick running in the background too, so a copy or a
file from your computer still reaches them while you use other apps: on
Android with a small "Ready to receive" notification (it also starts when
the phone starts; Settings → Keep running turns it off), on iPhone by
playing silence, which iOS shows as nothing and which mixes with your own
audio. An iPhone app swiped away from the app switcher stops anyway: iOS
allows nothing else.

Paired devices. A device can only do anything with yours after you pair
them with a 6-digit code or a QR code. Paired devices can then:
- ask to send you files (Sidekick asks you to Accept first, unless you turn
  off "Ask before receiving files" in Settings);
- browse your files and use your mouse and keyboard. Both are allowed by
  default; turn either off in Settings under "What paired devices can do
  here". Using the mouse and keyboard also needs the system's Accessibility
  permission on the Mac and Android, which you grant yourself;
- ping your device: it rings until you tap Found It! on it (on Android at
  alarm volume, so you hear it on silent);
- send you a link or a note. It's shown to you with Copy and Open (for a
  link), always, even from devices whose files you accept without asking.
  Sidekick doesn't keep it: it goes on your clipboard only if you tap Copy,
  and a link opens in your browser only if you tap Open.
Two things a phone can pass on to your paired Macs and PCs by itself, both
off until you turn them on:
- verification codes (Android only, Settings → "Send verification codes
  to computers"): when a new notification holds a one-time code (like
  "Your code is 482913"), only the code and the app's name go to your
  paired computers in reach, encrypted, where it shows in that phone's
  Chat with Copy code and as a notification. The rest of the message, and
  every notification without a code, never leaves the phone. Ongoing,
  private and system notifications are left out. Nothing is kept on the
  phone or sent anywhere else;
- its screenshots (Settings → "Send screenshots to computers"): each new
  screenshot goes to your paired computers in reach over Wi-Fi, encrypted,
  into their receive folder. The original stays in your Photos or gallery;
  the copy made for sending is deleted.
You can unpair a device at any time.

Encryption. Everything between paired devices is encrypted: over Wi-Fi with
TLS, checked against each device's own certificate; over Bluetooth with
AES-GCM keys agreed when you paired (SPAKE2, so the code itself never
travels).

PERMISSIONS, AND WHY

- Local network (all devices): to find and talk to your devices.
- Bluetooth (all devices): to connect when one of them isn't on Wi-Fi.
  While both are, Sidekick only uses Wi-Fi.
- Notifications (iPhone and Android): to show a file request while
  Sidekick is in the background, with Accept and Decline, and on Android
  its progress and the "Ready to receive" notice. Mac and Windows: to show
  your phone's verification codes and screenshots as they arrive.
- Background running (iPhone and Android): to stay reachable while you use
  other apps. Android may ask to stop optimising Sidekick's battery use
  (Settings → Keep running); the iPhone plays silence in the background.
- Camera (iPhone and Android): only to scan a pairing QR code. The picture
  is read on the device and is never saved or sent.
- Photos (iPhone): only the photos you pick to send, and adding the photos
  and videos you receive to your library. Adding doesn't let Sidekick see
  the rest of your library. If you turn on "Send screenshots to
  computers", iPhone and Android ask to read your photos, and Sidekick only
  looks for new screenshots.
- Notification access (Android): only if you turn on "Send verification
  codes to computers", which you allow yourself in Android's settings, to
  find one-time codes in new notifications and pass on only the code to
  your paired computers.
- Files and "All files access" (Android): to save files you receive and,
  if you allow it, let paired devices browse your files.
- Location (Android 12 and older only): those Android versions require it
  for Bluetooth and Wi-Fi scanning. Sidekick never reads or stores your
  location.
- Accessibility (Mac and Android): only so a paired device can move the
  pointer and type, when you allow it.
- Keep Wi-Fi awake (Android): while Sidekick runs in the background, so
  your devices can still reach the phone with its screen off. It asks for
  nothing and reads nothing.
- Direct Wi-Fi: when two devices share no Wi-Fi, one opens a small
  private Wi-Fi network (an Android phone's hotspot, or a Windows PC's
  Wi-Fi Direct network) and the other joins it (Android and iPhone ask
  you first). The network's name and
  password go only to the other device: over encrypted Bluetooth, or in
  the QR code shown on screen. It shares no mobile data, and closes when
  you're done.
- Firewall (Windows): the installer lets Sidekick accept connections from
  devices on private networks, and from the local subnet on public networks
  (for the direct link). Uninstalling removes these rules.

WHAT WE DON'T DO

- No account or sign-in.
- No analytics, tracking, advertising or crash reports.
- No servers: Sidekick never contacts us, and it doesn't go online to check
  for updates.
- We don't sell or share data. We don't have any.

THE WEBSITE

The wishlist. If you join it, the website keeps your email address, the
devices you picked (if any), and when you signed up. They're kept in a
private file on our web host (OVHcloud) and used only to email you: once
to say we got it, and once when Sidekick is out (we also get a short note
at our own mailbox about each new sign-up). They're never shared or sold. Every email has a link that removes your address at once, or reply
to it and ask. To stop the form being
abused, a scrambled (hashed) form of your IP address is kept for one hour;
the web host also keeps ordinary server logs.

Visits. The home page counts its visits: per day, how many times it was
opened, how many different visitors that was (each kept only as a short
scrambled code of your IP address and browser, mixed with that day's date,
so it can't be traced back to you or matched across days), and which other
website linked to it, if any. To spot abuse and see roughly where visitors
come from, it also keeps each visitor's IP address with the times it
visited and the browser it said it was, for 30 days. When we look at that
list, the addresses are sent to ipwho.is to show their country, city and
network. Nothing else, never shared.

Bot check. The site is for people only. A visitor that looks like a bot (a
bot's browser name, missing browser details, many requests a minute, or a
hosting or scanner network) is asked to tick "I'm not a robot" first.
Ticking it makes your browser solve a small puzzle on your own device; if
it passes, the site sets one cookie, sk_human, which says only that this
browser passed (signed, tied to the browser's name, for 30 days), so you
aren't asked again. To count requests a minute, the site keeps a scrambled
code of your IP address for one minute. If we switch on Cloudflare
Turnstile for this check, the check page loads it from Cloudflare, which
then sees your IP address and browser to tell people from bots (see
Cloudflare's privacy policy).

Apart from that one cookie, the website uses no cookies and no third-party
analytics. It remembers your light or
dark choice and color in your browser (local storage). It loads its fonts
from Google Fonts, so your browser connects to Google's servers (see
Google's privacy policy). If you install Sidekick through
another service, such as AltStore, SideStore, Sideloadly or the Microsoft
Store, that service's own privacy policy applies to it.

CHILDREN

The app doesn't collect personal data from anyone, including children. The
wishlist is for people 16 and over; if a child signed up, the link in the
email removes the address.
